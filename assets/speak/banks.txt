# Speak37's sentence banks: 16 sentences a bank, one per pad. Every key press speaks the next word of the pad's
# sentence (the sentence comes round again by itself: no line repeats itself). The jokes, the chants and the
# robots' lines are original text written for Speak37; SONGS and CHURCH are traditional or public-domain texts
# (their authors died more than 70 years ago). tools/gen_vocab.py speaks every word with espeak-ng and LPC-encodes
# it (TMS5220-style frames); the result is build/gen/sp_vocab.h. The USER bank comes after these (sp_user.c).
#
#   [BANK NAME]        a new bank (at most 12 characters on the screen), then 16 lines, one sentence each
#   [BANK NAME sing]   a sung bank: every word is sung on its key (SONGS: whole words, clearer than syllables;
#                      CHURCH: syllables); the player holds its vowel while the key is held, the rest when let go
#   [BANK NAME perc]   a bank of speech percussion: every word a hit (!WORD below: the sound, its length in ms)
#   voice=NAME         (in the brackets) the bank's own voice: choosing the bank chooses it
#   [KIT perc]         the percussion kit: 16 hits / their notes (KIT mode: a key or pad each), not a bank
#   de: fr: it: es: nl: sv: cmn: ja:   (a line's start) the line's language: espeak-ng's voice for it (a Portuguese
#                      bank was tried and left out: a speech recogniser understood none of its words as Speak37 says
#                      them, word by word)
#   ~                  (a line's first word) the line is sung, a word a key (in a spoken bank)
#   WORD{text}         the word (or syllable) said as text, espeak-ng's input ([[ ]] holds its phonemes)
#   WORD-WORD          one unit, said together on one key (PLUG-IT)
#   WORD = text        how a word is said;  ~WORD = text: how a sung syllable of CHURCH is sung
#   !WORD = text MS    how a hit sounds, MS its length (cut short and decaying: tight, no tail)
#   punctuation        kept on the screen, not spoken; a word is what lies between spaces
